Financial inclusion and mobile-money adoption: a survey-based analysis

Mayo T. N. Kadanga · FinScope Benin 2018 microdata

Research article in preparation. Empirical results and final interpretation will be added after source verification.

Research question. Which household and individual characteristics are associated with financial inclusion and mobile-money adoption?

1 Overview

This research article in preparation is designed for a study using FinScope Benin 2018 microdata to examine financial inclusion and mobile-money adoption. It sets out the intended structure for the completed analysis without presenting empirical findings.

The completed article will separate descriptive exploration, model specification, reproducible workflow and interpretation. It will avoid claiming representativeness, impact or causal effects unless those points are verified from approved sources.

2 Research question

The central question is: Which household and individual characteristics are associated with financial inclusion and mobile-money adoption?

The intended contribution is to organize a survey-based analysis that can connect socioeconomic characteristics, access patterns and digital finance adoption in a reproducible and reviewable format.

4 Methodological framework

The final study is expected to combine exploratory multivariate analysis with binary-choice modelling. The interpretation will focus on associations and, where appropriate, marginal-effect style summaries after the final model specification is verified.

Generic binary-choice model structure

Pr⁡(Yi=1∣Xi)=F(α+Xi′β) \Pr(Y_i = 1 \mid X_i) = F(\alpha + X_i^{\prime}\beta)

This equation is illustrative only. The final specification will be generated from the verified analysis source and will not use unverified variables in the public article.

7 Interpretation and policy relevance

The final interpretation must answer the following questions before any public conclusion is shown:

  • Which associations are stable enough to discuss publicly?
  • Which results are descriptive rather than causal?
  • What uncertainty, sample restriction or data-use boundary should accompany each interpretation?
  • Which findings can responsibly inform a policy or product discussion?

8 Limitations and ethics

This framework treats the analysis as secondary-data work. The completed article must document source limitations, sample restrictions, variable construction and disclosure boundaries.

The article will distinguish association from causality. It will not display personally identifiable information, raw private data, signatures, document numbers, or restricted institutional material.

9 Reproducibility statement

The intended pipeline is Quarto source plus R or Python analysis scripts, generated tables, generated figures and an environment or dependency record. Any public release of analysis materials is subject to data-use permissions and source verification.
